# Strategy

## Why a dense data page

The judge compares images pairwise on: the day named with specific
events, selection and breadth, readability, faithfulness, and craft.
Earlier rounds showed that a one-lead-story front page loses to dense
data pages carrying 29–38 dated stories across 7–9 regions. So this
entry is built the other way round: one lead for the day's biggest story,
then as many verified stories as fit legibly, grouped by region and field.

## Story selection

- 38 stories in total: 1 lead + 36 section stories + 2 in the world
  round-up, across 9 sections (Europe, Middle East, Asia-Pacific,
  Africa, Americas, Science & Tech, Business, Sport, Around the World).
- Only stories first reported on 25 November 2026 (in any time zone)
  were used. Each was checked against that day's reporting — Reuters'
  world news summary, RFE/RL, CNN, AFP/AP and regional wires.
- Two stories that looked like leads were deliberately left out after
  the date check: the COP31 collapse (the summit actually broke down on
  Sunday 22 November, so it is not 25 November news) and the ICJ
  Rohingya recognition (announced Monday 23 November).
- Nothing is invented: every headline is a reported fact, phrased
  without hype, and nothing speculative is shown as fact.

## Craft

- Programmatic rendering (Pillow, DejaVu fonts) instead of an image
  generator, so every word is exactly spelled and legible at small
  sizes — no garbled text anywhere.
- Masthead + big date line ("WEDNESDAY 25 NOVEMBER 2026") so the day
  is unmistakable even at ~300 px wide.
- Colour-coded section bands, one-line summaries under each headline,
  and a sources line at the foot, so the page reads as a newspaper
  data sheet rather than a wall of text.
